About Me

I am an experimental physicist and PhD researcher at Louisiana State University's Quantum Photonics Group, specializing in quantum photonics, plasmonics, optical materials, and quantum sensing. My research focuses on fundamental light–matter interactions and the experimental investigation of complex physical phenomena. I have experience designing, building, and optimizing advanced optical experiments, developing measurement approaches, and working with sophisticated photonic and quantum-optical systems.

Along the way, I have:

  • co-led the experiments behind quantum statistical plasmonic metacrystals, a room-temperature material that filters light by its quantum statistics, published in Nature (2026) and highlighted in Nature News & Views;
  • built a quantum-enhanced plasmonic hydrogen sensor that detects hydrogen ~100× faster and with a ~46× lower detection limit than the classical measurement (first author, Nature Communications, accepted in principle);
  • shown that multiphoton quantum coherence can emerge just by letting light propagate (first author, ACS Photonics, 2024).

In the lab I build, align, and characterize free-space and on-chip optical setups for quantum photonics experiments, build photon-number-resolving detection with SNSPDs and APDs, model nanostructures with FDTD and COMSOL, and automate data acquisition in LabVIEW and Python. Before LSU, I built and measured 3D-printed acoustic waveguides for my M.S. at the University of Texas Rio Grande Valley.

I'm looking for experimental R&D roles starting in 2027 where optical system design, quantum sensing and careful measurement can turn into new technology.

Outside the lab, I paint, garden and make crafts, and my favorite way to spend time these days is with our daughter.

Ongoing Projects

Project Lead

Quantum Reservoir Computing on a Plasmonic Platform

Leading experimental development of a plasmonic quantum reservoir for multidimensional information processing. The project combines orbital-angular-momentum and polarization control, SLM-based spatial-mode manipulation, photon-number-resolving detection with avalanche photodiodes, time-tagged photon measurements, and optical-correlation analysis.

Contributing

Plasmonic Biosensing

An on-chip plasmonic biosensing platform is being developed to characterize the refractive-index-dependent optical response of gold-based structures. Under collimated illumination, angular reflectance is measured with a photodetector. Coupling to a plasmonic mode produces a resonant reflectance minimum; tracking this feature across media with different refractive indices provides the sensing readout.

Contributing

Homodyne Phase Measurement

This work presents a tomography method that combines homodyne interferometry with photon-number-resolving detection to measure the quantum phase of multiphoton systems. It reconstructs complementary photon-number and phase representations for systems containing up to 15 photons, establishes the number–phase uncertainty relation, and enables spatially resolved quantum-phase characterization.

Contributing

Multiphoton Discrete Fractional Fourier Transform in a Waveguide Beam Splitter

Characterizing a waveguide beam splitter embedded in a glass plate to investigate the experimental realization of the multiphoton discrete fractional Fourier transform.
